# v4.4.6 — Live Risk Control & Emergency Response Engine

## 1. Objective

v4.4.6 defines the real-time safety control plane that operates after a certified build has entered live execution.

Its purpose is to detect, contain, degrade and recover from conditions that can create unsafe financial exposure.

The engine is independent from the ChanLun Decision Engine.

The Decision Engine determines what the strategy wants to do.

The Live Risk Control Plane determines whether the system is still allowed to do it.

## 2. Safety Principle

No strategy signal can override a live safety control.

The control hierarchy is:

Market/Data Safety
    ↓
Runtime Safety
    ↓
Position Safety
    ↓
Risk Limits
    ↓
Permission
    ↓
Execution

Emergency controls can interrupt this path at any point before financial side effects.

## 3. Control Plane Components

The Live Risk Control Plane contains:

1. Risk Monitor
2. Exposure Monitor
3. Market Data Guard
4. Position Protection Guard
5. Execution Health Guard
6. Reconciliation Guard
7. Signing/Permission Guard
8. Capital Policy Guard
9. Incident Detector
10. Kill Switch
11. Emergency Action Engine
12. Recovery Controller

## 4. Authority Separation

The following authorities remain separate:

Strategy
Risk
Permission
Capital
Signing
Emergency

Emergency authority can reduce or freeze execution but cannot increase capital authority.

Strategy has no emergency authority.

## 5. Real-Time Safety Loop

The runtime safety loop is:

Observe
  ↓
Normalize
  ↓
Evaluate Invariants
  ↓
Evaluate Risk
  ↓
Evaluate Incident Rules
  ↓
Determine Safety State
  ↓
Authorize / Restrict / Freeze
  ↓
Emit Audit Event

Every transition must be deterministic for the same input state and policy.

NORMAL
  ↓
DEGRADED
  ↓
RESTRICTED
  ↓
FROZEN
  ↓
RECOVERY
  ↓
SHADOW

A system MUST NOT transition directly from FROZEN to unrestricted PRODUCTION.

## 7. NORMAL

NORMAL permits the currently approved CapitalPolicy and execution scope.

Required:
- market data healthy;
- runtime healthy;
- state healthy;
- risk healthy;
- protection healthy;
- execution healthy;
- reconciliation healthy;
- signing healthy;
- policy hashes match.

## 8. DEGRADED

DEGRADED means a non-critical condition has reduced confidence but has not yet invalidated live safety.

Examples:
- temporary telemetry degradation;
- recoverable market-data reconnect;
- bounded latency increase;
- non-critical dependency outage.

Possible controls:
- reduce order rate;
- disable new positions;
- reduce maximum order size;
- increase observation requirements.

DEGRADED must have an expiration or recovery condition.

## 9. RESTRICTED

RESTRICTED prevents selected classes of new exposure.

Examples:
- stale market data;
- elevated exchange rejection;
- reconciliation lag;
- capital utilization near policy boundary;
- protection service degradation.

Typical controls:
- no new positions;
- close-only;
- reduce-only;
- lower leverage;
- lower notional;
- symbol-level disable.

Existing protection must remain active.

## 10. FROZEN

FROZEN prevents new financial exposure and blocks normal trading authorization.

FROZEN is required for conditions such as:
- unauthorized order;
- risk bypass;
- signing-policy violation;
- capital-policy violation;
- state corruption;
- unrecoverable reconciliation divergence;
- protection failure;
- compromised signing authority;
- detected lookahead in live path;
- critical position reconstruction failure.

FROZEN cannot be cleared by the Strategy Engine.

## 12. Hard Limits vs Soft Limits

Every risk control is classified:

### HARD_LIMIT
Violation causes immediate restriction or freeze.

### SOFT_LIMIT
Violation causes degradation or reduced authority.

The exact thresholds are deployment policy parameters.

They are not embedded in strategy code.

## 13. Exposure Guard

Before OrderIntent authorization:

proposed_exposure
<= approved_exposure_limit

and:

proposed_margin
<= approved_margin_limit

and:

proposed_leverage
<= approved_leverage_limit

If any mandatory condition fails:

DENY

The denial must produce a reason code.

## 14. Capital Guard

The capital guard validates:

capital_reserved
+ requested_reservation
<= capital_policy_ceiling

A strategy cannot bypass this check.

Capital policy reductions are immediately effective according to policy semantics.

## 15. Position Count Guard

The system must enforce:

current_open_positions
+ requested_new_positions
<= max_open_positions

The check must include pending orders where they can create additional exposure.

## 16. Order Rate Guard

The system tracks:
- orders per time window;
- cancellations;
- replacements;
- rejects.

A rate violation can trigger:
- throttling;
- symbol restriction;
- global restriction;
- freeze.

## 17. Loss Guard

The system must support:
- daily loss limit;
- rolling loss limit;
- maximum drawdown;
- per-position loss limit.

Loss controls must use authoritative realized/unrealized data as defined by the Risk Policy.

## 18. Market Data Guard

A market-data safety decision must evaluate:

freshness
+
sequence integrity
+
timestamp integrity
+
source health
+
instrument availability

If data is unsafe for a decision, the strategy cannot open new exposure.

A market-data failure must not disable existing protection unless a separately defined emergency policy permits it.

## 19. Stale Data

Each instrument has a maximum permitted data age.

If:

current_time - latest_valid_market_timestamp
>
policy.max_market_data_age

then:

NEW_ENTRY = DENIED

Further action depends on the protection and emergency policy.

## 20. Data Gap

For a detected sequence gap:

DATA_GAP
-> validate recoverability
-> if recoverable: DEGRADED
-> if not recoverable: RESTRICTED/FROZEN

The system must never fabricate missing candles.

## 21. Position Protection Guard

Every live position must have a valid protection state.

Protection state examples:

PROTECTED
PROTECTION_PENDING
PROTECTION_FAILED
PROTECTION_UNKNOWN

A required position with PROTECTION_FAILED or PROTECTION_UNKNOWN must trigger the policy-defined emergency response.

## 22. Stop Anchor Integrity

The control plane must verify that every protected position has:

- position_id;
- stop_anchor;
- stop policy version;
- creation timestamp;
- source decision;
- current protection status.

Stop-anchor mutation must be auditable.

## 23. Execution Health Guard

The guard monitors:
- reject ratio;
- acknowledgement latency;
- timeout ratio;
- unknown states;
- cancel failures;
- fill anomalies;
- slippage.

Thresholds are deployment parameters.

## 24. Unknown Execution State

UNKNOWN execution state is safety-critical.

Example:

Order submitted
-> acknowledgement lost
-> exchange state unknown

The system MUST NOT blindly submit the same order again.

Required sequence:

UNKNOWN
-> query authoritative state
-> reconcile
-> determine final state
-> only then permit retry if policy allows.

## 25. Reconciliation Guard

Reconciliation continuously compares:

Local State
vs
Exchange State

At minimum:
- position quantity;
- direction;
- average price;
- pending orders;
- fills;
- realized PnL where applicable.

Divergence classification:
- transient;
- recoverable;
- critical.

## 26. Reconciliation Failure

If a divergence cannot be resolved within the policy-defined window:

NEW_ENTRY = DENIED

If the divergence affects actual exposure or protection:

RESTRICTED or FROZEN.

The exact response is determined by the affected safety domain.

## 27. Slippage Guard

Execution must be checked against approved slippage policy.

If observed slippage exceeds the configured hard limit:

- reject future affected orders;
- reduce authority;
- or freeze according to policy.

Historical slippage is recorded for forensic analysis.

## 28. Abnormal Market Guard

The control plane may detect:
- extreme short-term movement;
- abnormal spread;
- liquidity collapse;
- price discontinuity;
- exchange mark/index divergence.

The response can be:
- reduce-only;
- no new positions;
- lower size;
- freeze.

Detection thresholds are deployment-specific.

## 29. Kill Switch

The kill switch is independent from strategy logic.

Activation sources:
- human operator;
- automated incident rule;
- security controller;
- capital controller;
- infrastructure safety controller.

Activation must:

1. stop new OrderIntent authorization;
2. prevent new live orders;
3. apply configured position action;
4. transition to FROZEN;
5. emit immutable audit event;
6. preserve evidence;
7. notify required operators.

## 30. Emergency Action Profiles

The system supports policy-defined emergency profiles.

### PROFILE_A — STOP_NEW
Disable new exposure.

### PROFILE_B — REDUCE_ONLY
Allow only position-reducing actions.

### PROFILE_C — CANCEL_NEW
Cancel unfilled new-entry orders where safe.

### PROFILE_D — FULL_FREEZE
Disable all normal execution.

### PROFILE_E — PROTECTIVE_EXIT
Execute pre-authorized emergency protection logic.

Emergency profiles are configuration/policy, not strategy decisions.

## 31. Emergency Action Ordering

Where multiple controls trigger simultaneously, the system follows the most restrictive applicable state.

Example:

NORMAL
+
RESTRICTED
+
FROZEN

Result:

FROZEN

Safety states are monotonic toward greater restriction within an incident until recovery is explicitly authorized.

## 32. Incident Correlation

Every safety transition references:
- incident_id;
- trigger_id;
- policy_version;
- build_id;
- config_hash;
- capital_policy_hash.

This creates a causal chain:

Signal
-> Rule
-> Safety State
-> Action
-> Position Effect

## 33. Multi-Trigger Handling

If multiple independent incidents exist, clearing one incident must not automatically restore NORMAL.

Example:

Incident A cleared
+
Incident B active

Result:

remain RESTRICTED/FROZEN according to B.

## 34. Recovery Conditions

Recovery from FROZEN requires:

1. root trigger identified;
2. affected state reconciled;
3. position protection verified;
4. capital policy verified;
5. build/config/policy identity verified;
6. no unresolved critical incident;
7. evidence preserved;
8. recovery approval recorded.

Recovery then proceeds:

FROZEN
-> RECOVERY
-> SHADOW
-> TESTNET
-> MICRO_CANARY

## 35. Automatic vs Manual Recovery

### Automatic
Allowed only for explicitly classified low-risk transient conditions.

### Manual
Required for:
- unauthorized order;
- signing violation;
- capital violation;
- state corruption;
- protection failure;
- critical reconciliation divergence;
- security incident.

## 39. Idempotency

Emergency actions must be idempotent.

Repeated:

KILL_SWITCH_ACTIVATED

must not create multiple conflicting emergency actions.

Repeated risk evaluation of the same state must produce the same authorization result.

## 40. Concurrency

The control plane must handle simultaneous triggers safely.

Example:

MarketDataStale
+
RiskLimitBreached
+
ReconciliationDivergence

must converge to one effective safety state.

The most restrictive applicable action wins.

## 41. Failure of the Risk Controller

The system must define behavior when the Live Risk Control Plane itself fails.

Fail-safe default:

NO_NEW_EXPOSURE

If the risk controller cannot prove that an order is safe, the order cannot be authorized.

Existing protection follows the emergency policy.

## 42. Failure of Observability

Telemetry failure must not automatically imply trading safety.

If canonical safety state and durable audit remain available, the system may enter DEGRADED according to policy.

If safety evidence required for authorization is unavailable, new exposure must be denied.

## 43. Failure of State Store

If authoritative state cannot be reconstructed:

NEW_ENTRY = DENIED

If actual position cannot be established:

RESTRICTED/FROZEN according to exposure risk.

## 44. Failure of Exchange Connectivity

When exchange connectivity is lost:

- new entries are denied;
- pending order state becomes UNKNOWN where appropriate;
- authoritative reconciliation is required before retry;
- existing protection follows the emergency policy.

## 45. Security Invariants

I1 — Strategy cannot disable live risk controls.
I2 — Strategy cannot increase capital authority.
I3 — Kill switch is independent of strategy.
I4 — Unknown execution state cannot cause blind retry.
I5 — Missing market data cannot create a new position.
I6 — Missing authoritative state cannot authorize new exposure.
I7 — Protection failure cannot be silently ignored.
I8 — FROZEN cannot self-clear.
I9 — Emergency controls cannot increase authority.
I10 — Every emergency action is auditable and idempotent.

## 46. Observability Integration

-> new_ceiling

new_ceiling <= old_ceiling

## 54. Exit Criteria

v4.4.6 passes when:

1. Live Risk Control Plane is independent from Strategy.
2. NORMAL/DEGRADED/RESTRICTED/FROZEN states work.
3. Hard and soft limits are supported.
4. Market-data guard works.
5. Exposure and capital guards work.
6. Position protection guard works.
7. Unknown execution handling works.
8. Reconciliation guard works.
9. Kill switch works independently.
10. Emergency actions are idempotent.
11. Multiple triggers converge safely.
12. Risk-controller failure fails closed for new exposure.
13. Recovery requires explicit evidence.
14. Emergency incidents feed forensic replay.
15. Emergency incidents can create regression cases.
16. All emergency actions are auditable.

## 55. Scope Boundary

v4.4.6 does not define specific numerical risk thresholds for a particular exchange, asset or account.

It defines the real-time safety control architecture that enforces those policy parameters and prevents strategy logic from bypassing them.
